These two twenty-four-year-olds offered to sell their entire company for less than the price of a modest house.
The boardroom laughed them out of the building.
The algorithm was PageRank.
Those students were Larry Page and Sergey Brin.
And the boardroom that rejected them was Yahoo.
Here is the part nobody understands:
Yahoo didn't reject Google because the technology was bad.
They rejected it because it was too fast.
In nineteen ninety-eight, Yahoo's entire revenue model was built on trapping you on their portal for forty-five minutes to sell banner ads.
Google built the exact opposite: an engine designed to give you an answer and kick you off their site in three seconds.
Yahoo called that a distraction.
Four years later, in two thousand and two, Yahoo realized their mistake and offered three billion dollars in cash.
Larry and Sergey said five.
Yahoo walked away a second time over two billion dollars.
Fourteen years later, Yahoo sold their core business to Verizon for scrap metal.
Meanwhile, that rejected search algorithm became a two trillion dollar empire.
Passing on the future once is a mistake.
Passing on it twice is an extinction event.
