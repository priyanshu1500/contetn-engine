In June 2005, two 22-year-old college grads were kicked out of Y Combinator.
Their startup was a mobile food ordering app on old flip phones.
Paul Graham hated it and told them to go home.
On the train back to Virginia, their phone rang.
It was Graham: "Your idea is dead. But we like you. Build the front page of the internet."
Steve Huffman and Alexis Ohanian got off at the next station, turned around, and rented a dingy Cambridge apartment.
Steve coded the first version of Reddit in just 20 days.
The problem: When they launched, the site was completely dead. Zero visitors.
So Steve and Alexis built an admin panel and created hundreds of fake ghost profiles.
Every single morning, they submitted links, upvoted themselves, and had full debates under fake names just to make the front page look alive.
Visitors showed up, thought it was a thriving community, and started posting.
16 months later, Condé Nast bought Reddit for $10 million dollars. They were 23.
Today, Reddit is a $15 billion dollar public empire, and the primary dataset powering Google and OpenAI's artificial intelligence.
Every digital empire you use today started with two kids in a living room faking it until it became the internet.
