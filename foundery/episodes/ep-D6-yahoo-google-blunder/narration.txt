Yahoo had two chances to buy Google.
In 1998, for one million dollars.
In 2002, for three billion.
They said no both times.
The first rejection is the one that changed the world.
Larry Page and Sergey Brin walked into Yahoo headquarters and offered them the entire PageRank algorithm for one million bucks.
Yahoo laughed them out of the boardroom.
Their reason?
Google's search engine worked too fast.
It sent users off the site in three seconds.
Yahoo made money by keeping people trapped on their portal, clicking banner ads and reading horoscopes.
Google built their entire trillion-dollar empire on the exact opposite bet:
Give people the answer instantly, and get out of their way.
Yahoo didn't miss Google by accident.
They actively rejected the idea that won.
Here is the warning for every business owner today:
Right now, legacy companies are rejecting autonomous AI workflows for the exact same reason:
It collapses their billed hours.
The market doesn't care about your billable hours.
The faster machine always wins.
